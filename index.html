Overview

THE OBJECT is an interactive installation projection featuring two suspended jellyfish-like organisms that explore the relationship between humans and opaque versus transparent technologies.

Through slow, responsive movement, soft illumination, and projection, the work invites participants to reconsider how they engage with digital systems—not as tools of speed and efficiency, but as objects that require patience, attention, and care.

Form & Material

The installation features two suspended jellyfish constructed from translucent 3D printed TPU. The material choice shifted from biomaterials due to the need for structural reliability and the integration of electronic components.

While the initial proposal focused on soft materiality, the final work emphasizes conceptual clarity—using material as a support system for exploring technological visibility and interaction.

Open vs. Black Box

Black Box Jellyfish

A smooth, enclosed form that conceals its internal mechanisms. Its behavior appears responsive but mysterious, reflecting consumer technologies that prioritize usability while hiding complexity.

Open Jellyfish

A semi-exposed structure that reveals motors and internal systems. Movement becomes legible, inviting curiosity and understanding rather than passive consumption.

Interaction

The jellyfish respond to proximity and touch through embedded electronics. Light, movement, and projection shift in response to participant engagement.

Responses are intentionally slow and gradual. Quick interactions yield little feedback, while sustained engagement produces richer visual and physical responses.

The projection extends the interaction into the surrounding space, translating behavior into fluid, ambient visuals that evolve over time.

Themes

Slowness

The installation counters fast-paced consumerism by requiring participants to move at the pace of the jellyfish, encouraging presence and sustained attention.

Empathy

Empathy is reframed as patience, curiosity, and understanding. Participants engage with technology not as tools, but as entities that require time to comprehend.

Transparency

By contrasting open and concealed systems, the installation questions how visibility affects trust, curiosity, and emotional connection to technology.

Experience

Set in a dim, quiet space, the installation creates an immersive environment of soft light, subtle sound, and slow movement. The jellyfish gently sway, setting the rhythm of the room.

Participants are invited to slow down, observe, and reflect—transforming interaction into a sensory and temporal experience rather than an immediate response system.
