The Holodeck Installation

The final exhibition realized Arus Daryayi as a performance-based environment. By placing one creature on the ground—tethered by yarn—and another suspended, we created a visual narrative of the "Black Box" versus the "Open System."

Circuit & Logic

The Movement Language

We transitioned from servos to stepper motors to achieve the high torque and precision required for the fishing wire "tendons." The motor sequencing includes:

  • Breathing: A rhythmic grouping of four legs.
  • Bioluminescence: Fading internal lights triggered by ultrasonic distance.
  • Joy of Touch: Full extension of legs wrapping toward the hand when touch is detected.

The 2-2 Conversing Model

The system utilizes an MPR121 with 12 electrode pins mapped to 4 stepper motors. The interaction is a negotiated agreement; if a participant releases the leg mid-motion, the creature "homes" (uncurls), requiring the user to match the artifact's internal effort and pacing.

Reflections & Discoveries

The "Black Box" iteration served as a physical manifestation of technical friction. By attempting to force electronics into a sealed form under a strict deadline, we engaged in the very culture of fast production we intended to critique. The failure of the soldered circuit within the Black Box highlighted how concealment creates "functional ignorance."

Conversely, the Open Jellyfish (the halfway misprint) allowed for intuitive assembly and easier troubleshooting. We discovered that transparency enhanced empathy, as participants could observe the physical effort of the motors. This honest relationship between creator, participant, and machine became the project's most successful realization.

Future Directions

Future iterations will explore a hybrid structure—a TPU skeleton for reliability coated in a stabilized gelatin skin to reclaim the organic aesthetic of the initial bioplastic research. We also aim to develop synchronized motor sequencing that allows the two jellies to "talk" to each other through light and kinetic movement.
